Why another model?

Every frontier AI today carries three taxes: a key-value cache that grows until phones choke, one-token-at-a-time thinking, and weights that take seconds to load. Olai attacks all three from India, on a zero budget — by refusing to train at all.

Instead of burning crores on GPUs, Olai's engine REA (Recursive Extraction & Assimilation) reverse-engineers the trained weights of open-source models — Liquid LFM2.5, SmolLM2, DeepSeek-R1 distills, Qwen, Gemma-class — reads them as knowledge packets, and merges the best of each into one compact edge model. The weights are already trained; we just re-pack them.

The REA pipeline

1

Inspect

Fingerprints any safetensors checkpoint: architecture, 40+ functional roles, spectral stats (σ-max via power iteration).

2

Harvest

Pulls selected tensors from any Hugging Face model over HTTP range requests — DeepSeek's FFN slices in 165 MB, not 3.5 GB.

3

Assimilate

Projects foreign subspaces across architectures (approx-SVD top-r, relative-depth alignment) into the Olai anchor.

4

Pack

Emits .olai: 4096-aligned packets, 64-byte fixed-stride binary directory, native int4 quantization, per-packet CRC32.

What survives the merge — and what doesn't

We measured donor deltas before trusting them: LFM2-Math/ENJP checkpoints carry deltas 3.4× larger than the weights themselves (cos similarity between their deltas: 0.21 — near-orthogonal). Merging them poisoned the model; even a 10%-weight soup failed. So Olai merges same-basin only: the LFM2.5 anchor, true LoRA-scale donors, and cross-architecture FFN subspaces — each step gated by live evals.
